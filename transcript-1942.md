# Family narrative attributed to Eva Horner, 1942

**v21.0 | Source S04 | Published excerpts and later reconstruction, not a complete diplomatic transcript**

The full manuscript, authorship/date and transmission chain have not been verified. Earlier excerpts in this repository describe Spencer being shot after surrendering, William receiving a head wound, a story about hidden money, and later reprisals attributed to Dr. John Turner Horner. These are claims made by the family account, not independently established findings.

The published excerpt states: “One time they left Jess Wilson to hold their horses when they went to fight” [S04]. The surrounding published passage does not identify “they” or specify which fight is meant.

## Reported transmission chain

The earlier repository page attributes the speaking voice to Prof. John Turner Horner, identifies “Dad” as his father, Dr. John Turner Horner, and names Eva Horner, the professor's daughter, as recorder in 1942. On that attribution, this is the professor reporting what his father said, rather than Eva's eyewitness testimony about 1864. These roles and the transmission chain have not been checked against the complete original manuscript.

## Context in the published excerpt page

The sentence immediately before the horse-holding sentence is “Dad said Jess Wilson was a brave man too.” The two preceding excerpt blocks describe a road killing and a dance-house raid and name Wash Middleton. An earlier excerpt names Wash Middleton and Fate Arnold in an attributed squad account. These are the website's arranged excerpts, not verified consecutive manuscript sentences. “Too” suggests a comparison but does not identify its referent. The heading “Squad Members — Additional Detail” was editorial framing on the earlier website; it is not used here to identify Wilson's party. Its occurrence in the original manuscript has not been verified.

A claim that seventeen of twenty attackers were killed in reprisals is sole-source family tradition here. Exact identities, dates, motives, locations and casualty totals remain open. The source does not establish what investigation or prosecution occurred. Read alongside the current source catalog and research register.
