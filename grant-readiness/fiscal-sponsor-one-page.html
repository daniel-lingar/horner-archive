Institutional project brief

Prepared for discussion | 17 October 2026 | No committed institution

Records, Memory, and Civilian Violence: The Horner Family in Civil War Arkansas would be a temporary public exhibition developed and led by an eligible institution in collaboration with descendant researcher Daniel Bret Lingar and outside historians.

The project asks how census and land records, a memorial, and later family narratives illuminate different parts of civilian wartime experience. It avoids certifying unverified perpetrators, motives, parcel locations or prosecution history. Existing resources include a corrected census image, patent, source catalog, current reading edition and companion lesson prototype. Independent interpretation, selected-source verification, rights, design and public testing remain proposed work.

The illustrative 12-month planning request is $25,920, with rates and quantities documented as estimates. NEH Public Humanities Projects, Exhibitions: Planning has a 16 December 2026 deadline. An individual or LLC cannot apply; a fiscal sponsor acting for an ineligible organization is also excluded. The prospective institution must adopt its own substantive project and approve staff, advisers, rights, audience plan and budget. No school, scholar, venue or archive has committed.

Requested next discussion: whether this public-history question fits the institution’s mission and capacity, and whether it would lead a planning application with agreed decision-making and a properly scoped researcher role. This is not a request merely to receive and pass through funds.
